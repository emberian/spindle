# RIG — The Rules

*Enough to understand a match and follow a broadcast. Not a regulation
rulebook — the Reg is ten thousand pages and three dead commissioners.*

Novel terms are **bold** at first use and collected in the Glossary
(§10). The deliberate simplification pass — the coined term vs. the
single word actually shouted on the field — is §11, because the user is
right: nobody yells "antispinward" in the middle of a match.

---

## 1. The object of the game

Move the **bell** (the ball) through the other crew's **goal ring** more
than they move it through yours, across **nine innings**, in the
weightless **calm** at the center of a spinning habitat. There is no
clock. The game ends when the innings are spent, not before, not after.

## 2. The field

- The **calm**: the weightless tube on the spin axis. A regulation field
  is **640 m long** along the axis, sleeved by a soft mesh **skin** at a
  fixed radius. Touch the skin and you are **grounded** — out of the
  play until you re-enter from your own end.
- **Gravity is a slope.** Dead center: weightless. Toward the **skin**
  (rimward): heavier, faster, more committed. The slope is in play at
  all times. Going **deep** (rimward) buys power and speed but commits
  you. Going **high** (axisward) buys time and options but makes you
  slow and readable. *Deep is faithful, high is free.*
- **The two ends are not equal.** The world spins one way. The goal you
  attack *with* the spin is the **Faith** end — the honest, heavier,
  easier score. The goal you attack *against* the spin is the **Free**
  end — the world's own crookedness fights you there, so it is rarer and
  worth more. The pre-match choice of ends is the single most important
  decision in rig.
- Down the axis run fixed **spars** (clip-points). The field is divided
  by three notional **gates** — **first**, **deep**, **mouth** — the
  last just short of the goal ring.

## 3. The gear

- The **bell**: a dense, soft sphere that **rings** when it spins true
  and **clatters** when it tumbles. You read it by ear. Knocking it off
  true is called making it **clatter**.
- The **line**: every player carries a powered tether-and-claw — a
  grapple. **In the calm you cannot fly.** You change direction only by
  pushing off a spar/teammate or by throwing your line and hauling. The
  line is your legs, your brakes, and your craft. Mastering line work
  (swings, hauls, slingshots off an anchored teammate) is most of the
  skill of rig.

## 4. The crew

Six in the calm per side; free substitution at any dead bell. Positions
are **radius jobs**, not field positions:

| Pos | Name | Job |
|----|------|-----|
| 1 | **Anchor** | Plays deep/heavy. Power and last defense. Never floats high. |
| 2 | **Spinners** ×2 | Midfield engine. Trade altitude for tempo, run the lines, set loop plays. |
| 2 | **Wings** ×2 | A **Faithwing** (power, spinward side) and a **Freewing** (artist, against-spin side; the franchise's signature). |
| 1 | **Reach** | Goal-ring keeper. May clip and pivot on the ring itself. |

## 5. How play runs — casts, counts, innings

**The cast (the football part).** A possession is a **cast**. The
attacking crew gets **three throws** to carry the bell past the next
**gate**. Clear a gate → fresh three throws. Fail to clear a gate in
three → turnover at that spot. Radius is a free extra direction of
advance or escape — a cast moves goalward *and* up/down the slope at
once.

**The contest, on a count of three (the baseball part).** The instant a
defender contests a bell in flight, play **freezes** into a one-on-one:
thrower vs. contester, lines live, everyone else locked at their last
anchor. It resolves over **three exchanges** ("the count"):

- Thrower wins the count → **complete**; the cast continues from the
  catch.
- Contester **snatches** (takes it clean) or makes it **clatter** and
  recovers → turnover, *at that radius and direction* — and that
  matters: a contest fought **deep** is fast, violent, final; one fought
  **high** is slow, reversible, a chess game. The slope is inside the
  duel, not just around it.

A rig box score is a wall of contest lines — who completed against whom,
at what radius, which direction. That is the stat-haunted baseball soul,
in flight.

**The inning.** An inning ends on a score or a turnover-and-clear. A
match is **nine innings**. Tie after nine → **spine**: sudden-death
single casts at the Free end, against the world's hand, until someone
scores. No clock, ever.

## 6. Scoring

| Score | Worth | What it is |
|-------|------:|------------|
| **Fall** | **2** | Bell cleanly through the **Faith** (spinward) ring. The honest score. |
| **Rise** | **5** | Bell through the **Free** (antispinward) ring, *into* the world's wrong-handed curve. Most players never score one in a season. |
| **Loop** | **7** | Score with a **closed Coriolis arc**: the bell leaves a hand, is touched by no one, bends through the calm on the world's own math, and falls through a ring. *Ends the inning immediately.* The founding trick, weaponized — rig's home run, no-hitter, and Hail Mary at once. The crowd goes silent to hear if the bell is still ringing. |
| **Ground** | **1** (defense) | Force an attacker into the skin. The grinding soul of great defensive habitats. |

## 7. Fouls (the short list)

- **Garrote** — putting a live line *across* another player's flight or
  the bell's flight path. The cardinal sin. Ejection; the cast is the
  victim's.
- **Cording** — wrapping a line on an opponent's body or line (vs.
  legally clipping a spar/ring). Turnover + advance.
- **Skinning** — deliberately driving an opponent into the skin with
  contact. Turnover; repeat = ejection.
- **Crowding the Reach** — contacting the keeper while they're clipped to
  the ring. Free undefended throw from the mouth.
- **Slow bell** — refusing to set a live bell to run nonexistent clock.
  There is no clock; pretending there is, is a foul. (This rule exists
  because three commissioners tried to add a clock. The league enjoys
  this.)

## 8. Officials

Three: the **Spar** (calls casts, gates, sets), the **Ear** (rules the
bell true or clattered — *by sound*; the Ear is blindfolded by tradition
in title matches), and the **Reach Judge** (the goal-ring). Disputes go
to the Ear. The Ear's hearing is, canonically, the most insured organ in
professional sport.

## 9. The No-Clock Law

Stated plainly because it defines the sport's feel: rig has no clock, no
shot timer, no period, no stoppage time. It ends when nine innings of
casts are spent. A blowout still plays nine; a classic still ends at
nine; the only thing that runs long is a tie, which can run *forever*.
This is not a quirk. It is the pastoral, step-outside-of-time baseball
soul, and the league guards it like scripture.

## 10. Glossary — the novel concepts a newcomer must be told

These are the ideas with no Earth equivalent; everything else is just
vocabulary.

- **The calm** — the weightless volume on a habitat's spin axis. The
  field is a *volume*, not a plane.
- **The slope / spending radius** — effective gravity rises smoothly
  from zero at the axis to full at the rim. Your distance from the axis
  is a resource you continuously spend: *deep* = power+speed+commitment,
  *high* = time+options+softness. No Earth sport has this axis.
- **The asymmetry (Faith vs. Free)** — because the world spins, the two
  goals are physically different. One is honest and cheap (**Fall**),
  one is against-the-grain and dear (**Rise**). Choosing which end you
  attack is the deepest strategy in the game.
- **The Coriolis curve / the loop** — thrown things bend, and bend
  *hard the other way* when thrown against the spin, enough to fly a
  closed arc back where they started. The founding trick and the
  7-point score.
- **The line** — you cannot fly in zero-g; a grapple is the *only*
  mobility. Rig is a grappling-hook sport at the bones.
- **Reading by ear** — the bell rings when true and clatters when
  tumbled, so the definitive sense in rig is *hearing*, which is why the
  sport broadcasts on audio across light-years (see economics).

If a newcomer holds those six, they can follow any match.

## 11. The simplification pass (coined term → what's actually shouted)

The user's note, honored: a designed term and a *spoken* term are
different objects. Left column is what the document/the Reg calls it;
right column is the single word a rigger yells mid-flight. The right
column is canonical *on the field*; the left is canonical *on paper*.

| Designed / on paper | Shouted on the field | Note |
|---|---|---|
| the calm | **the calm** | already one word; kept |
| rig line / tether-grapple | **line** | "throw line!", "I'm on line" |
| the bell (ball) | **the bell** | kept; the verb is "ring it" / "clatter it" |
| spinward (with the spin) | **fair** | "go fair", "fair side" |
| antispinward (against the spin) | **cross** | "he's going cross!" — the glamour word |
| rimward / toward the skin | **deep** (or "down") | "take it deep" |
| axisward / toward the axis | **high** (or "up") | "he's high and free" |
| the Faith end / Faithful goal | **Faith** | "attacking Faith" |
| the Free end / Free goal | **Free** | "they're a Free team" |
| a cast (possession) | **a cast** | kept; "fresh cast" |
| a contest / the one-on-one | **a one** | "we're in a one", "won the one" |
| the count (3 exchanges) | **the count** | "down to the count" |
| make the bell clatter | **clatter** (verb) | "clatter it!" — defense's oldest cry |
| take it clean | **snatch** | "snatched at the mouth" |
| Faithwing / Freewing | **the Faith / the Free** | a star Freewing is just "their Free" |
| the goal ring | **the ring** | |
| grounded (into the skin) | **skinned** | "he got skinned" |
| extra spine (overtime) | **spine** | "we're going to spine" |
| the Spindle Regulation | **the Reg** | |
| a regulation match | **a rig** | "good rig tonight"; you "rig up" |

**The principle, stated for the GM:** every glamorous thing has a short,
ugly, monosyllabic field word (**cross**, **loop**, **clatter**,
**snatch**, **skinned**, **a one**) because rig was named by tired
people on a long shift who needed to be understood across a vacuum gap
on a bad audio link. The jargon's *bluntness is itself canon* — it's the
riggers' fingerprint on the language, the same way "town ball" and "the
gridiron" carry their sports' origins. Keep the blunt words; they're
true.
