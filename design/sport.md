# RIG — The Sport

*The game played in the calm at the heart of a spinning world.*

Formal league name: **the Spindle League** (the sport is *rig*; a match
is *a rig*; you *rig up*; a player is *a rigger*). Naming rationale and
the full simplified glossary live in `design/rules.md`; this document is
the *design* — what the game is, where it came from, and how it pays for
itself.

---

## 1. The room the universe already built

Every O'Neill cylinder has a dead spot. Spin the world to make weight at
the rim and the long axis down the center stays weightless — O'Neill
drew it on the first napkin as *the recreation volume*. Riggers call it
**the calm**: a windless, weightless tube tens of kilometers long and a
few hundred meters across, running the spine of a habitat that holds
tens of millions of people. The sky is the far land curving up and over
your head; the ground is everywhere and below you at once; and right
here, on the axis, nothing falls.

Rig is the only major sport whose field is a *volume*, whose floor is a
*direction*, and whose physics are *locally true science fiction*. You
do not learn rig by learning rules. You learn rig by un-learning the
ground.

### The three facts that are the whole game

**1. Gravity is a slope you spend.** Effective weight scales with how
far you are from the axis. Dead center: nothing. Halfway out: a lean,
loping lightness. Near the rim: home, full weight, the world you grew up
on. A rigger trades altitude for advantage constantly. Drop *rimward*
and you get speed, mass, and a punishing arc — but you have committed;
the slope does not give refunds. Float *axisward* and you get time,
options, and reversibility — but you are slow, soft, and easy to read.
Coaches say it in three words: **deep is faithful, high is free.**

**2. The calm is handed, and the hand is cruel.** A spinning world
deflects everything that flies through it. Throw *spinward* (the way the
world turns) and the ball arcs roughly the way your gut expects. Throw
*antispinward* (against it) and the ball **rises, curves, and can sail
clear around a hard bend** before it ever comes down. The whole sport is
lopsided around this. One end zone is *downhill in the world's logic*;
the other is *uphill against it*. Offense in one direction is a
different sport than offense in the other, and every great rigger has a
strong side the way a pitcher has a strong arm.

**3. You cannot fly. You can only let go, and grab.** In the calm there
is no swimming, no walking, no cutting. You change your vector exactly
two ways: you **push off something solid**, or you **throw a line and
haul.** Every rigger carries a **rig line** — a powered tether-and-claw
descended directly from the construction grapples the game was born
with. The line is your legs, your brakes, your bond to your team, and
the single most beautiful and dangerous object on the field. Rig is, at
its bones, a grappling-hook sport. The hook is not a gimmick bolted on.
The hook is the *only reason humans can play at all*, and the riggers
knew that before anyone wrote it down.

## 2. Where it came from (the organic origin)

Nobody designed rig. Riggers did, the way water designs a canyon.

Cylinders are not built from the rim in. They are *spun up around a
spine*. For years before a habitat has land, weather, or weight, it has
a turning skeleton and crews living in the calm at its center —
**riggers**, the tethered construction gangs who walk the spine in
vacuum and pressure shifts, clipping and unclipping, hauling mass
through nothing on powered lines, eating and sleeping and getting bored
in the one part of the structure that will never have gravity.

Bored people with tethers and a long shift invent a bet. The first bet,
every old crew swears it was theirs, was the **loop**: throw your
ballast bag up-and-against-the-spin so the world's own crookedness
curves it in a closed arc and drops it back into your glove without you
moving your feet. Anyone gravity-raised thinks it is a trick. It is just
arithmetic, and the riggers were the first humans in history for whom
that arithmetic was *home cooking*. Loop contests became loop-and-snatch
(someone else tries to take it out of the air). Snatch became *keep it
away from their side of the spine*. Keep-away got a thrown line involved
because you could not chase without one. Keep-away with lines, two crews,
a marked-off stretch of spine, and a tool bag for a ball — that is rig,
and it existed for a generation before it had a name, the way baseball
was just *town ball* and football was just a riot with a leather egg.

It kept three things from its birth that it never lost, because they
were never decisions:

- **No clock.** A shift ends when the work is done, not when a horn
  blows. Rig ends when the *innings* (we will get there) are spent. The
  game is pastoral on purpose by being pastoral by accident — it is
  baseball's clockless, weatherless, *step-outside-of-time* soul, and it
  got that soul from men and women waiting out a long build.
- **Improvised gear.** A ball is anything with the right mass that hurts
  a little but not too much. A field is any clipping points you agree
  on. The barrier to a pickup rig is *a tether you already own and four
  friends*. This is why it spread like a cough — the same reason
  basketball needed a peach basket and nothing else.
- **The diaspora's two religions, fused.** The crews were ex-Americans,
  mostly: Frontier Republic riggers, Commonwealth riggers, Belt
  contractors, Concordat-flagged Lira-paid gangs working everyone's
  hulls. They brought football's whole grammar of *territory, downs,
  the set piece, the end zone* and baseball's whole grammar of *the
  duel, the count, the stat line, the long quiet afternoon* — and the
  calm forced them to braid the two into one body, because in
  weightlessness a scrum is suicide and a one-on-one in mid-air is
  glorious.

## 3. The game itself

### 3.1 The field — *the rig*

A regulation rig is a length of the calm, **640 m long along the axis**,
bounded by two **goal rings** — wide hoops centered on the axis at each
end. The volume is sleeved by a soft mesh **net wall** at a fixed radius
(the *skin*) so play stays in the calm and off the curving land; touch
the skin and you are *grounded* (out of the play, must re-enter from
your own line). Down the spine run fixed **spars** — the structural
clip-points every cylinder already has — that players and lines anchor
to. The two ends are not equal: by rule the spinward goal is the
**Faithful** end (its scoring arc bends *with* the world, the easier,
heavier, more honest direction) and the antispinward goal is the **Free**
end (you must score *against* the world's hand — rarer, prettier, worth
more). Choosing ends is the coin toss that matters in rig, and it is the
deepest strategic choice in the sport.

### 3.2 The ball — *the bell*

A dense, soft-shelled sphere about the heft of a full waterskin, cored
with a low ring of mass so it **chimes when it spins true** and goes
silent when it tumbles. Riggers read the bell with their ears as much as
their eyes — a clean pass *rings*, a wobbled one *clatters*, and
defenders are trained to break the chime. "Make it clatter" is the
oldest defensive instruction in the game.

### 3.3 The crews

Six a side in the calm at once (deep benches; substitution is free at
any dead bell, baseball-style). The positions are not Earth positions —
they are *radius jobs*:

- **The Anchor (1).** Plays deep, near the rim end of the gradient,
  heavy and fast. Your hammer and your last line of defense. The Anchor
  almost never goes high; an Anchor caught floating is a coach's
  nightmare.
- **Spinners (2).** The midfield engine. They live in the slope, trading
  altitude for tempo, running the lines, setting the *loop plays.* The
  best riggers in the league are Spinners.
- **Wings (2).** Specialists by side — a **Faithwing** (devastating
  spinward, the power direction) and a **Freewing** (the artist;
  antispinward, the impossible-angle scorer). A team's Freewing is its
  signature; ticket sales have Freewings' names on them.
- **The Reach (1).** The goal-ring keeper, but mobile — defends the
  scoring arc, and is the one player allowed to *clip the ring itself*
  and pivot on it. Reaches are the sport's lunatics.

### 3.4 The core loop — *casts and the count*

Rig is not run by a clock; it is run by **casts**. A cast is one
possession sequence, the way a *drive* is in football, and it is scored
inside a **count** the way an at-bat is in baseball. Both DNAs are load-
bearing, not decorative.

A cast begins with the bell live at a spar (a *set*). The attacking crew
has **three throws** to move the bell goalward past staged **gates**
(three notional rings dividing the field — *first water, deep water,
mouth*). Reaching the next gate refreshes you to three throws; failing
to advance a gate in three throws turns the bell over at that spot. So
far: football's downs, in three dimensions, with *radius* as a free
extra axis of advance and retreat.

The baseball is in the **contest**. Any time a defender contests a live
bell in flight, play freezes into a **one-on-one duel** on **a count of
three**: thrower and contester, alone in the calm, lines live,
everyone else frozen at their last anchor. The thrower is trying to
*complete*; the contester is trying to *clatter* (knock the bell off
true) or *snatch* (take it clean). Three exchanges decide it — pull
the bell true on the count and the cast continues from the catch; lose
the count and it is a turnover *with the gradient consequences of
wherever you fought it*. Duels fought deep (rimward) are violent and
fast and final; duels fought high (axisward) are slow, chess-like,
reversible — the gradient is *in the duel*, not just around it. This is
the unit fans actually come for. A rig box score is a wall of duel
lines: who completed against whom, at what radius, in which direction.
It is *baseball's stat-haunted intimacy*, in flight.

### 3.5 Scoring

- **A fall — 2.** Put the bell cleanly through the *Faithful* (spinward)
  goal ring. The honest score. The bread.
- **A rise — 5.** Put it through the *Free* (antispinward) ring — *into*
  the world's wrong-handed curve. Most riggers never score one in a
  season. A rise changes a city's mood.
- **A loop — 7 and the cast ends the inning.** Score using a *closed
  Coriolis arc*: the bell leaves a rigger's hand, is touched by no one,
  bends through the calm on the world's own math, and falls through a
  goal ring. The founding trick, weaponized. A loop is the sport's home
  run, no-hitter, and Hail Mary fused into one event, and stadiums of
  forty thousand go *completely silent* while a loop is in the air
  because the bell's chime is the only way to know if it is still true.
- **A ground — 1, to the defense.** Force an attacker into the skin.
  Small, grinding, and the soul of a great defensive habitat's identity.

A match is **nine innings**; an inning ends on a score or a turnover-
plus-clear. Tie after nine → **extra spine**: single cast, sudden, at
the Free end, against the world's hand, forever until someone is brave
or stupid enough. There is no clock. There has never been a clock.
There will never be a clock. (Three separate Spindle League commissioners
have died proposing one. The league counts this as a rule.)

### 3.6 Why it is deep

The strategic spine is the **end choice + the gradient + the
asymmetry**, interacting:

- The team holding the Faithful end scores easier but cheaper; the team
  attacking the Free end is chasing 5s and 7s into a headwind of
  physics. Whole franchise philosophies fall out of which bargain a
  habitat's character prefers (Section 6, and the whole of the League
  document).
- Every throw is a *radius decision* and a *direction decision* at once.
  Going deep buys power and forecloses options; going high buys options
  and forecloses power. The greatest Spinners are the ones who keep the
  question open the longest.
- The rig line turns it into an arena-movement sport on top of a
  ball sport: line physics (swing, haul, slingshot off a teammate's
  anchored line, the illegal-if-caught *garrote* across a flight path)
  are a whole craft. Lasertag-fast, Xonotic-mobile, but every motion
  costs you a clip you might need later.
- The bell's chime makes the *whole crowd a sensor.* Rig is the rare
  sport more legible by ear than by eye, which is why it radio-cast
  across light-years before it ever video-cast well.

## 4. Codification (the boring miracle that made it a sport)

A pickup game becomes a *sport* the day two groups who have never met
need to play each other fairly. Soccer needed public schools to write
rules so rival schools could meet. Rig needed something harder, and the
something was **physics itself.**

Here is the engineering problem that *is* the history of rig: **no two
cylinders spin alike.** A big slow Frontier agricultural cylinder and a
small fast Belt spin-station have *different gravity gradients,
different Coriolis strength, a different-shaped calm.* A loop that
closes perfectly in one habitat sails wide in another. For a generation
that did not matter, because crews only played their own spine. The
moment a Frontier crew and a Commonwealth crew wanted to play *for
something*, rig had a crisis no Earth sport ever faced: **the field
itself is a different field on every world.**

The fix is the most important document in the sport's history, the
**Spindle Regulation** (riggers just say **the Reg**): not a rulebook
first but a *spec* — a defined regulation arena (the 640 m length, the
fixed skin radius, *a mandated reference spin*) that any habitat large
enough must be able to reproduce inside its own calm with a sleeved,
counter-trimmed practice spindle, so that the game plays *identically*
on Tau Ceti and in the Belt and around Wolf 359. The Reg is rig's metric
system. The political fight over *whose* spin became the reference (it
ended up a negotiated value nobody's home habitat actually uses, which
is very rig and very Concordat — see Section 6) is the sport's founding
myth and its first great rivalry.

The codification arc, compressed:

1. **Folk era.** Crew rig, house rules, no two spines alike. Decades.
2. **The Bag Codes.** First written rules, per-habitat, so crews on one
   cylinder could run a real season. Mutually incompatible. The
   "translation matches" between habitats were chaos and are now
   beloved folklore.
3. **The Reg.** The standardized arena spec + unified rules. The
   *single act* that turned a thousand local games into one sport. Rig
   historians date "the sport" from the Reg the way football dates from
   the codified rules, not from the first riot with an egg.
4. **The Spindle League.** A governing body to run the Reg, certify
   regulation spindles, and crown a champion across systems —
   association-football's FIFA arc, at interstellar scale, and (this is
   the Vivere Astra hook) one of the very few institutions FRM, CNE, and
   the Concordat all sit on without anyone getting bombed.

## 5. Evolution into an interstellar sport

Rig globalized the instant the O-drive made *travel* cheap before it
made *anything else* cheap.

- **The radio age.** Because the bell *chimes*, rig was the first sport
  that broadcast across a star system on nothing but audio — a habitat
  could not afford to ship video but could afford a man with a good
  voice and a chime to react to. Rig calls became an art form. The great
  system-spanning rivalries were *heard* before they were ever seen, and
  the league's oldest revenue is the **call rights**, not the gate.
- **The jump leagues.** Once O-drive made crews ship-able, every settled
  system that had a regulation spindle could host. The league bent into
  a structure no Earth sport had: **conferences by light-distance**, a
  travel economy where a road trip is *a jump and a month*, and a
  championship where the trophy physically crosses interstellar space
  and is, itself, a security event. (Ananke has tried for a champion-
  ship spindle once. The league does not discuss it. NISO does not
  either.)
- **The Reg as soft power.** Certifying a regulation spindle is
  effectively recognition that a habitat *belongs.* Frontier and
  Commonwealth use spindle certification the way 20th-century powers
  used Olympic hosting; the Concordat, with no hard power, *runs the
  certification body* — because the one thing the Lira-broker
  civilization is trusted to do is hold a neutral reference value and
  not cheat with it. Rig's metric system is Concordat-kept. This is
  funny and load-bearing, exactly like the Vatican-guaranteed Lira.
- **Why people still play it.** Borrowed straight from 17776's thesis:
  in a century of aggie ghosts, relativistic-killer-mystery dread, and
  three human powers one bad day from war, rig is the thing a scattered,
  frightened, post-collapse humanity does *together on purpose, with no
  clock, for no reason except that the chime is still ringing.* The
  sport is the diaspora telling itself it is still one people. That is
  the canon pitch to Kanzo: rig is not set dressing. Rig is **what
  Vivere Astra's humans have instead of a shared sky.**

## 6. The sport as a culture-carrier (bridge to the League)

A habitat's *physics* shapes its *style*, and its style shapes its
*soul*. This is the engine of the 32-team document; here is the engine
spec:

- **Big slow cylinders** (Frontier agricultural worlds, old Sol giants):
  shallow gradient, gentle Coriolis. Play is **patient, deep, Faithful-
  end power rig** — long casts, heavy Anchors, the *fall* as a virtue.
  These habitats produce dynasties and bore neutrals.
- **Small fast spin-stations** (Belt, frontier outposts, Luhman
  orbitals): savage gradient, vicious Coriolis. Play is **high, fast,
  Free-end chaos** — chasing 5s and 7s, all Freewing, gorgeous and
  unstable. These habitats produce legends and heartbreak.
- **Mid cylinders** (Concordat Tau Ceti, Commonwealth core, Sol
  mid-stock): balanced. Play is **the line game** — tether craft,
  tempo, the broker's rig. Concordat habitats famously play *for the
  draw and the away point*, which everyone finds infuriating and which
  wins them road trophies.
- **The named asymmetry as identity.** Whether a habitat's culture
  prefers the *honest cheap Fall* or the *vain expensive Rise* is, in
  the fiction, a genuine read on that society — Frontier worlds are Fall
  cultures, Belt worlds are Rise cultures, the Concordat is a *ground*
  culture (win ugly, win away, hold the reference), France's habitats
  (NPC) are an aesthetic of forced Rises the league quietly considers
  decadent and dangerous.

## 7. The financials of rig

Rig's economics are *strange in exactly the way the setting is strange*,
and Kanzo can hang real campaign hooks on them.

### 7.1 The revenue stack (top to bottom)

1. **Call rights (the biggest line).** Audio broadcast rights, sold per
   star system, jump-delayed. Because a live system-to-system feed is
   physically impossible (lightspeed), rig sells **the call, not the
   game** — and the *re-call*, where a system's own famous voice
   re-performs a jumped-in match from the chime log days later, is the
   single most valuable media product in human space. Rig is the only
   sport where the *broadcast is a cover version* and that is normal.
2. **The gate & the spine.** Live attendance, sold by *radius band*:
   rim-side seats (heavy, cheap, loud, the soul) up through axis-side
   boxes (weightless, expensive, where the franchise's money sits and
   the deals get done). A rig stadium's price gradient *is* the field's
   gradient. On-the-nose, intended, beloved.
3. **Spindle certification & the Reg.** The Concordat-kept governing
   body charges habitats to certify regulation spindles and audits the
   reference value. This is small money and enormous power.
4. **The bond market on casts.** Rig has no clock, so rig has *no
   garbage time and no kneeling the ball* — every cast is live value.
   This made rig the substrate for the largest legal **in-play wagering
   market** in the settled systems, denominated, of course, **in
   Lira**, because the Lira is the currency that already runs in the
   seams between everyone's flags. Rig betting is a meaningful fraction
   of why the Concordat's currency stays liquid in places the Concordat
   flag never flies. *This is a direct, deliberate hook into Vivere
   Astra's stated Concordat economics.*

### 7.2 The costs (where the campaign hooks are)

- **Travel is the budget.** A road trip is an O-drive jump: exotic
  matter, weeks of crew downtime, real Jump cost (the canon currency).
  Rich systems (Frontier XM surplus, Commonwealth depth) field
  travel-heavy contenders; the Concordat fields a *broker's franchise*
  that wins on points and away draws because it cannot afford to lose
  jumps. **A team's schedule is a logistics problem the size of a
  colonial supply line.** Kanzo can make a rig road trip *a lead.*
- **Spindle upkeep.** A counter-trimmed regulation spindle is heavy
  industry. Habitats that can build and certify one are flexing
  Industry; habitats that lose certification (sabotage, riot, neglect)
  lose a *civic organ.* Ananke has learned that a regulation spindle is
  a soft target with enormous symbolic yield — a sabotaged spindle is a
  depopulated stadium and a humiliated faction without a single
  publicized death. NISO treats spindle security as infrastructure
  security. **This is a ready-made misdeed/lead surface.**
- **Riggers are scarce skilled labor.** A top Freewing is a person who
  can do, by trained instinct, the Coriolis arithmetic that the aggies
  used to do by being aggies. The league's quiet anxiety — never said on
  a broadcast — is that the best riggers are valuable for *exactly the
  spatial-intuition reason France recruits spacers and Ananke recruits
  the high-intelligence young.* The pipeline from rig academy to
  someone's astronautics corps to someone's terror cell is a real,
  unspoken thread. The sport is a talent fountain everyone is drinking
  from. **Hook, fully loaded, handed to the GM.**

### 7.3 The shape of the money, in one line

Rig is a **clockless, audio-first, Lira-denominated, jump-bottlenecked**
sport whose governing metric is held by the one faction with no army —
which is to say rig's economics are Vivere Astra's economics with a ball
in them. That is the argument for canon.

## 8. Design summary (carried into Rules, Phase 3)

- Venue: the calm; gradient is a spent resource; asymmetry (Faithful vs
  Free / Fall vs Rise) is the strategic spine.
- Movement: rig line (grapple) is the only mobility; arena-shooter craft
  on a ball-sport skeleton.
- Structure: casts (football downs, 3D, +radius) resolved through
  contests (baseball duels on a count), nine innings, **no clock**.
- Ball: the bell; legibility is *auditory*; this drives the media
  economy.
- Scoring: Fall 2 / Rise 5 / Loop 7 (ends inning) / Ground 1 (defense).
- Identity engine: physics → style → soul → faction, for the League doc.
- Economics: call rights, radius-banded gate, Concordat-kept Reg, Lira
  in-play market, jump-cost schedule, spindle-as-soft-target,
  rigger-as-dual-use-talent.
