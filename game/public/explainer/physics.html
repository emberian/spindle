Why it's a real problem

Coriolis is not a special effect. It's the game.

RIG is set in a rotating habitat because a rotating frame is a genuine non-inertial control domain — the same class of problem that makes spinning spacecraft hard to move around inside of. The curves are not animation. They fall out of the equations of motion.

What the rotating frame does to you

Stand in a spinning cylinder and you feel two fictitious forces. Centrifugal pushes you outward, growing with radius as ω²r — zero at the axis, about 0.8 m/s² at play radius, a heavy 4.6 m/s² at the 45 m skin. Coriolis acts sideways on anything moving in the spin plane, bending straight intentions into arcs.

So the calm is not "just weightless." Near the axis it is nearly weightless and nearly fair. Drift outward and the world starts pulling and turning under you. Aim where the bell is and you will miss; you have to aim where the frame is going to take it.

The one axis that tells the truth

Here is the detail the whole sport is built around: motion along the cylinder's axis is inertial. No Coriolis acts on it. Move up the tube and you move straight; move across the spin and you curve.

That asymmetry is the strategic spine. The calm has an honest direction and a deceitful one, and good play is knowing, in your hands, which is which at every instant.

Across the spin, the world lies to you. Along the axis, it doesn't.

The Loop, drawn

A straight push, seen from inside the spinning frame, comes back curved. Time it so the curve closes through a ring and you have a Loop — a shot that orbits to its target along a path only this frame could produce.

Honest dynamics under the hood

None of this is faked with eased keyframes. The bell is a rigid body with angular momentum, integrated as a symmetric top — proper gyroscopic dynamics, precession and all — stepped with RK4 in the rotating frame so Coriolis and centrifugal terms enter the integration, not a shader. Spin it clean and it holds its axis; strike it and it wobbles, exactly as a tumbling object should.

And it is exactly repeatable

This is the part that turns a sport into a laboratory. The simulation is bit-exact deterministic: one seed in, one identical match out, every time, on every machine. Same inputs, same trajectory, down to the last bit of the last float.

That means a "lucky bounce" is reproducible, a strategy can be compared against itself with nothing else changed, and a highlight is also an experiment. Determinism is what makes any claim about this swarm got better mean something.

Determinism is not a feature here. It's the instrument.
