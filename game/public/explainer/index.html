A sport — and a lab

RIG is a sport that doesn't exist, built to study a problem that does.

On the surface: a zero-gravity grappling sport played in the calm — the weightless near-axis volume of a spinning space habitat. Underneath: a deterministic, bit-exact, watchable laboratory for getting four agents to cooperate against a thinking opponent. Every match is a controlled experiment you can replay frame-for-frame — and watch like a highlight reel.

Two true things at once

RIG is a real sport with real culture — a bell you read by ear, a money shot called the Loop, a 32-team league and a bracket called The Jump. It is also a serious control problem wearing a sport's clothes: partial information, sparse structured reward, an adversarial side, and physics that lie to your intuition.

The trick is that both halves are the same artifact. The thing you watch for fun is the thing the researcher measures — because the simulation is deterministic to the bit. Nothing about it is staged.

A sport you can watch. A lab you can replay. The same engine, exactly.
